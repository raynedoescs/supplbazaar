Essentials

Choline

The cell-membrane nutrient.

Choline is an essential nutrient. The body makes a little of it, but not enough, so it also has to come from food.

Daily reference425–550 mg
Upper limit3,500 mg
Top food sourcesEggs, meat, fish, soybeans
Most at riskPregnancy, low animal-food diets
What it doesHow muchFoodsLow levelsToo muchMedicinesTesting

What it does

  • Cell membranesNeeded to make two fats that are vital parts of cell membranes.
  • Brain and nervesUsed to make acetylcholine, a messenger for memory, mood and muscle control.
  • LiverHelps move fat out of the liver.

How much you need

WhoDaily reference
Men · 19 and over550 mg a day
Women · 19 and over425 mg a day

These are Adequate Intakes. Needs are higher in pregnancy and breastfeeding, and most pregnant women eat less than this.

General healthy-adult daily reference, not a supplement dose. Needs vary by age and life stage. Upper limits are not intake targets.

Eggs are one of the richest everyday sources of choline.

Who is more at risk, and signs of low levels

Who is more at risk

  • Pregnant women, most of whom eat less than the reference
  • People with certain genetic variants that raise choline needs
  • People fed only through a drip into a vein (parenteral nutrition)

Signs that can go with low levels

  • Muscle damage
  • Liver damage
  • Fatty liver not caused by alcohol (NAFLD)

Although most people eat less than the reference, true deficiency in healthy men and non-pregnant women is very rare, because the body makes some choline.

Too much

The US adult upper limit is 3,500 mg a day from all sources. Too much can cause a fishy body odour, vomiting, heavy sweating, low blood pressure and liver toxicity. This limit is not a target.

Medicines that can interact

  • No known interactionsNIH lists no clinically relevant interactions between choline and medicines. Still mention any supplement to your doctor or pharmacist.

Never stop or change a medicine because of what you read here. Ask your doctor or pharmacist.

How it is measured

There is no simple routine test for choline status. A clinician looks at diet and, where relevant, liver tests.
