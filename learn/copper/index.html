Copper

The energy and iron-helping metal.

Copper is an essential mineral. It works as part of enzymes (cuproenzymes), and most copper in the blood is carried by a protein called ceruloplasmin.

What it does

  • EnergyPart of the enzymes that produce energy in cells.
  • Using ironHelps the body move and use iron to make red blood cells.
  • Connective tissue and nervesNeeded to build connective tissue and to make some brain chemical messengers.

How much you need

WhoDaily reference
Everyone · 19 and over900 mcg a day

Needs are higher in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Ask your clinician.

General healthy-adult daily reference, not a supplement dose. Needs vary by age and life stage. Upper limits are not intake targets.

Most varied diets provide enough copper. Amounts vary by variety and preparation.

High-dose zinc can lower copper: get tested before considering a copper supplement.

Who is more at risk, and signs of low levels

Who is more at risk

  • People with coeliac disease
  • People who take high-dose zinc supplements or use a lot of zinc-containing denture cream
  • Children with Menkes disease, a rare genetic condition

Signs that can go with low levels

  • Anaemia
  • Lighter skin or hair
  • Weaker bones
  • Problems with balance and coordination
  • More frequent infections
  • High cholesterol

Low copper is uncommon. These signs have many other causes.

Too much

The US adult upper limit is 10,000 mcg (10 mg) a day from all sources. Too much copper over time can damage the liver and cause stomach pain, nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea. This limit is not a target.

Medicines that can interact

  • Zinc supplementsNo common medicine interactions are listed, but high-dose zinc, including zinc-containing denture creams, can block copper absorption and lead to low copper.

Never stop or change a medicine because of what you read here. Ask your doctor or pharmacist.

How it is measured

Blood copper and ceruloplasmin (the protein that carries most copper). A clinician interprets them together.

A clinician can decide which tests are useful and interpret them with your symptoms and diet. Blood tests do not always reflect the body's nutrient stores.
