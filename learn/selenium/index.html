Selenium

The thyroid-protecting mineral.

Selenium is an essential mineral that forms part of about 25 proteins (selenoproteins). How much is in plant foods depends on the soil they grew in.

What it does

  • Thyroid hormonesSelenoproteins help the body make and use thyroid hormones.
  • Protection from damageHelps protect cells from oxidative damage and infection.
  • DNA and reproductionInvolved in making DNA and in reproduction.

How much you need

WhoDaily reference
Everyone · 19 and over55 mcg a day

Needs are slightly higher in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Ask your clinician.

General healthy-adult daily reference, not a supplement dose. Needs vary by age and life stage. Upper limits are not intake targets.

Common in Indian kitchens

  • Fish and seafood
  • Eggs
  • Chicken
  • Whole grains
  • Dairy

Amounts in grains and vegetables vary a lot with the soil where they were grown. A single Brazil nut can provide more than a day’s reference.

Low selenium rarely causes illness on its own, and too much is harmful: get tested before considering a supplement.

Who is more at risk, and signs of low levels

Who is more at risk

  • People living in regions with selenium-poor soil
  • People on kidney dialysis
  • People living with HIV

Signs that can go with low levels

  • Usually no symptoms on its own
  • In very low-selenium regions, linked to a heart-muscle disease (Keshan disease) and a joint and bone disease (Kashin-Beck disease)
  • It may make the effects of low iodine on the thyroid worse

Low selenium mainly matters when another stress, such as an infection or low iodine, is also present.

Too much

The US adult upper limit is 400 mcg a day from all sources. Too much causes hair and nail loss, garlic-smelling breath, a metallic taste, rashes and nerve problems. Just 1 ounce of Brazil nuts (6–8 nuts) can contain 544 mcg, more than the upper limit. This limit is not a target.

Medicines that can interact

  • CisplatinThis chemotherapy medicine can lower selenium levels. Anyone having cancer treatment should ask their oncologist before taking selenium.

Never stop or change a medicine because of what you read here. Ask your doctor or pharmacist.

How it is measured

Blood or plasma selenium. A clinician interprets it with your diet, health and where you live.

A clinician can decide which tests are useful and interpret them with your symptoms and diet. Blood tests do not always reflect the body's nutrient stores.
