Zinc

The immune and healing mineral.

Zinc is an essential trace mineral. You need only small amounts, but it is used by hundreds of enzymes throughout the body.

Daily reference8–11 mg
Upper limit40 mg

What it does

  • Immune functionHelps your immune system work.
  • Wound healingNeeded for skin repair and wound healing.
  • Growth and cell buildingUsed in making DNA and protein and in cell division.
  • Taste and smellNeeded for a normal sense of taste and smell.

How much you need

WhoDaily reference
Men · 19 and over11 mg a day
Women · 19 and over8 mg a day

Needs are higher during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Ask your clinician.

General healthy-adult daily reference, not a supplement dose. Needs vary by age and life stage. Upper limits are not intake targets.

Zinc from some plant foods is less readily absorbed.

Who is more at risk, and signs of low levels

Who is more at risk

  • People with bowel conditions, or who have had weight-loss (bariatric) surgery
  • People who follow vegetarian or vegan diets
  • People with alcohol use disorder
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women, who have higher needs

Signs that can go with low levels

  • Diarrhoea
  • Hair loss
  • Slow wound healing and skin changes
  • Frequent infections
  • Changes in taste or smell
  • In children: slower growth

Signs vary by age and have many other causes. Changes in taste or smell should not be treated as proof of low zinc.

Too much

The upper limit for adults is 40 mg a day from all sources, including zinc lozenges and cold products. Too much zinc over time can lower copper levels and harm nerve and immune function. This limit is not a target.

Medicines that can interact

  • Quinolone and tetracycline antibioticsZinc can reduce how much of these antibiotics your body absorbs, so doses are usually spaced apart.
  • PenicillamineZinc can reduce absorption of this medicine.
  • DiureticsSome water tablets increase zinc lost in urine.

Never stop or change a medicine because of what you read here. Ask your doctor or pharmacist.

How it is measured

No single test shows zinc status reliably. Blood zinc is used, but a clinician also considers diet, symptoms and risk factors.

A clinician can decide which tests are useful and interpret them with your symptoms and diet. Blood tests do not always reflect the body's nutrient stores.
