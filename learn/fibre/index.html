Fibre

The plant food your gut runs on.

Dietary fibre is the part of plant foods that your body cannot digest. It passes through the gut, where it adds bulk and feeds helpful gut bacteria.

WHO daily targetAt least 25 g
Fruit and vegetablesAt least 400 g
Top food sourcesPulses, whole grains, veg
Most at riskProcessed, low-plant diets

What it does

  • Regular digestionAdds bulk and softness to stools, helping keep bowel movements regular.
  • Feeling fullFibre-rich foods are filling and slow down digestion.
  • Healthy dietsWHO advises replacing refined carbohydrates with fibre-rich whole grains, fruit, vegetables and pulses.

How much you need

WhoDaily reference
Adultsat least 25 g a day

This is a WHO target for everyone over 10 years old. Fibre from whole foods counts; WHO’s target is for naturally occurring fibre.

General healthy-adult daily reference, not a supplement dose. Needs vary by age and life stage. Upper limits are not intake targets.

Where fibre comes from

  • Pulses: dal, chana, rajma and beans
  • Whole grains: atta, oats, brown rice and millets
  • Vegetables
  • Whole fruit (more fibre than juice)
  • Nuts and seeds

Common in Indian kitchens

  • Dal and chana
  • Rajma
  • Atta roti and daliya
  • Millets: ragi, bajra, jowar
  • Seasonal fruit and vegetables

Swapping refined grains (white rice, maida) for whole grains and adding a pulse to most meals are simple ways to eat more fibre.

Whole foods are the best sources; increase fibre gradually and drink enough fluid.

Who is more at risk, and signs of low levels

Who is more at risk

  • People whose diets are mostly processed or fast foods
  • People who eat few fruits, vegetables, pulses or whole grains
  • People who often miss meals or eat very little

Signs that can go with low levels

  • Constipation: hard or difficult-to-pass stools
  • Going three or more days without a bowel movement
  • Straining on the toilet

Fibre isn’t measured by a blood test, and constipation has many causes, including fluids, activity and medicines. See a clinician about lasting changes in bowel habit, blood in stools or weight loss.

Too much

There is no upper limit for fibre from food. Increasing fibre quickly can cause wind and bloating, so increase it gradually and drink enough fluid.

Medicines that can interact

  • Fibre supplements and medicinesSupplements such as psyllium can change how some medicines are absorbed. Take them at a different time and ask your pharmacist.

Never stop or change a medicine because of what you read here. Ask your doctor or pharmacist.

How it is measured

There is no blood test for fibre. A clinician looks at diet and bowel habits, and investigates any lasting change.

Constipation has many causes, including fluids, activity and medicines. See a clinician about blood in your stools, weight loss or a lasting change in bowel habit.
