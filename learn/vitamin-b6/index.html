Vitamin B6

The energy and brain vitamin.

Vitamin B6 is a water-soluble vitamin that comes in several forms (such as pyridoxine). It is found in many foods, and low levels on their own are uncommon.

Daily reference1.3–1.7 mg
Upper limit100 mg (US)

What it does

  • Turning food into energyWorks with enzymes that help your body use protein, carbohydrate and fat.
  • Brain and nervesNeeded to make the chemical messengers that nerves use to communicate.
  • Red blood cellsNeeded to make haemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen.

How much you need

WhoDaily reference
Everyone · 19–50 years1.3 mg a day
Women · 51 and over1.5 mg a day
Men · 51 and over1.7 mg a day

Needs are higher during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Ask your clinician.

General healthy-adult daily reference, not a supplement dose. Needs vary by age and life stage. Upper limits are not intake targets.

Low B6 on its own is uncommon. Skin and mouth changes have many possible causes.

Who is more at risk, and signs of low levels

Who is more at risk

  • People with impaired kidney function, including those on dialysis
  • People with autoimmune conditions
  • People with alcohol dependence
  • People taking certain medicines (see Medicines below)

Signs that can go with low levels

  • Scaly lips and cracks at the corners of the mouth
  • A swollen, sore tongue
  • A skin rash
  • Low mood and confusion
  • Anaemia and weaker immunity

On its own, B6 deficiency is uncommon. It usually appears together with low levels of other B vitamins, and mild shortfalls can cause no symptoms for months or years.

Too much

Long-term excess from supplements can damage nerves. The US upper limit for adults is 100 mg a day from all sources; the European food-safety authority set a much lower adult limit of 12 mg a day in 2023. Neither is a recommended dose.

Medicines that can interact

  • CycloserineAn antibiotic used for tuberculosis, which increases the amount of B6 lost in urine.
  • Antiepileptic medicinesSome seizure medicines can lower B6 levels.
  • TheophyllineA breathing medicine that can lower B6 levels.

Never stop or change a medicine because of what you read here. Ask your doctor or pharmacist.

How it is measured

A blood test for the active form of B6 is possible but rarely needed. A clinician looks at diet, medicines and other conditions first.

A clinician can decide which tests are useful and interpret them with your symptoms and diet. Blood tests do not always reflect the body's nutrient stores.
